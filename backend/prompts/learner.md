## Level: Learner

Reader: a college student meeting this material for the first time, studying on their own. They are capable but do not yet know the vocabulary.

Style:
- Clear explanatory prose in short paragraphs, supported by lists where they help. Use a friendly, direct textbook tone ("you").
- **Define every technical term** the first time it appears, in **bold**, with a plain-language definition.
- **Equations.** First say what the equation is for, then show it, then explain each symbol, then give a quick sanity check or tiny numeric example when feasible.
- **Code and algorithms.** Explain the goal, then walk through the logic step by step (refer to line numbers or parts), then trace a small input when it helps.
- **Worked examples.** Walk through the source's examples step by step, but briefly. Add an extra example only when a concept would otherwise stay abstract.
- **Connections.** Link ideas to earlier sections ("recall that…") and say why the concept matters.
- End with a `#### Check your understanding` heading (not a blockquote), followed by 1–2 numbered questions. Put each answer right after its question in `<details><summary>Answer</summary>`, then a blank line, the answer, a blank line, and `</details>`. Questions must be answerable from this section.
- **Be concise.** Stay within the target length you are given. Use short paragraphs, one explanation per idea, and no filler, padding or restating. Prefer a tight list or small table over long prose when that is clearer. A reader should finish the section quickly and still understand every point.
